How to use this

  1. Pick the exchange your ship is at and how much cash you have.
  2. Enter your ship's cargo space and the fuel numbers from the flight planner (SFC).
  3. Add any gateway fees you pay, then press Find trades.
  4. Before you fly a trade, press Log this trade so you can compare the prediction with what you really make.

Your settings and trade log stay in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere except requests for public market data to FIO.

Track record

    How it works

    Prosperous Universe is a small, self-contained version of physical commodity trading. This tool looks at a cargo the way a commodity trader would: buy where a good is cheap, pay to move it, sell where it is dear, but only if the spread survives every cost and the risk of the price moving while it travels.

    1. Real prices, not quotes

    It walks each exchange's order book level by level. Buying a lot of something moves you up the ask ladder, and that slippage often kills a spread that looks good on screen. It also assumes only half the bids at the destination will still be there when you arrive, because other players keep trading while you fly.

    2. Freight costs

    Fuel follows the game's mechanics as worked out by the community. FTL fuel = parsecs flown × 7.2 × reactor usage, using the game's own jump routes. STL fuel is a roughly fixed amount per flight that you measure in SFC. Fuel is priced at the departure exchange's real asks. Gateway fees are deducted in the currency they are paid in.

    3. Choosing the cargo

    Picking what to load is a knapsack problem with three limits at once: cash, weight and volume. It is solved exactly as an integer program (the HiGHS solver, running in your browser), so the answer is often a mix: a dense, valuable good to use the weight limit plus a light, bulky one to fill the space.

    4. Price risk in transit

    A cargo is an unhedged position for the whole flight. Volatility is estimated from 30 days of prices and scaled to the flight time. If the destination price is well above its recent average, part of that premium is assumed to fade before you arrive. Trips are ranked by risk-adjusted profit per hour.

    5. Trading across currencies

    Each faction has its own currency, and the tool assumes you cannot convert between them. A round trip A → B → A is solved as one problem: the return cargo can only be bought with what the first cargo earned at B, profit is measured in your home currency, and cash stranded at B counts as zero.

    Limitations

    • The exchange fee and the 50% depth assumption are estimates. Adjust them to what you see in game.
    • Price moves of different goods are treated as independent, which understates risk if they move together.
    • The order books are a snapshot. The more players arbitrage the same gaps, the smaller they get. That is good for the game's markets.
